Seating guide

Escort cards vs place cards

One tells a guest which table. The other tells them which chair. Most weddings need both, and the interesting question is not what they are — it is how to stop them contradicting each other.

The difference in one line

An escort card waits at the entrance and sends a guest to a table. A place card waits on the table and sends them to a seat.

Escort card Place card
Where it sits A table near the entrance At the place setting
What it says Name and table Name only
Sorted by Surname, A–Z Table, then seat
One per Guest Seat
You need it when Guests have assigned tables Guests have assigned seats

Do you need both?

If you assign tables, you need something at the door — escort cards or a large seating chart sign. Without it, 150 people stand in the entrance reading a list over each other's shoulders.

Place cards are the second decision, and a smaller one: they only matter if you are assigning specific seats rather than just tables. Assign seats when there is a plated dinner with meal choices, when the table has people who should not be opposite each other, or when a table of ten would otherwise leave one person taking whatever chair is left. Otherwise, let the table sort itself out.

A seating chart sign replaces escort cards. It does not replace place cards.

What to write on them

  • Formal: titles and surname — "Mr Baker" — unless two guests would then read the same, which at a wedding they usually would.
  • Everything else: first and last name, matching how you addressed the invitation.
  • Couples: one escort card between them is fine if they are seated together; place cards are always one per person, because they mark chairs.

Alphabetise escort cards by surname. It is how a guest searches for their own name in a rack of two hundred, and sorting by first name is the single most common way to make that rack unusable.

The mistake that costs an evening

Made separately, the two sets drift apart. The escort cards get printed on Tuesday. On Thursday someone cancels, a table is rebalanced, and the place cards are printed from the corrected list. Now one guest is at table 4 on the door and table 7 on the plate, and nobody finds out until they are standing there holding a card.

This is not carelessness — it is what happens whenever the same information is typed twice. The fix is not to be more careful. It is to generate both sets from one plan, so they cannot disagree.

Questions people ask

Which comes first when a guest walks in?

The escort card. It is the only one they see before they know where they are going.

Can I use place cards without escort cards?

Only with a seating chart sign at the entrance. Place cards are invisible until you are already at the right table, so on their own they leave everyone stranded at the door.

What about a guest who does not come?

Remove the escort card before the doors open — a card left in the rack tells the whole room who did not turn up. The place card matters less; an empty setting reads as an empty setting.

How many do I print?

One escort card per guest, one place card per assigned seat. If you are printing on Avery 5302 tent stock, that is four cards a sheet — see the Avery 5302 measurements.
