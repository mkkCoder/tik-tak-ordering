Seating guide

How many people fit at a 60-inch round table?

Eight comfortably. Ten if you squeeze, and everyone will know it. Here is the arithmetic that gives you the answer for any table size, and what the tenth chair actually costs.

The short answer

A 60-inch round table seats eight guests with room to eat, and ten if you are short of space and willing to trade elbow room for it. Caterers and rental companies quote eight; wedding venues quote ten because it lets them sell the room to more people.

Both numbers are right. They are answers to different questions.

Where the number comes from

Guests sit around the edge, so what matters is the circumference, not the area. A person needs roughly 24 inches of table edge — enough for a place setting and two elbows. That gives one calculation that works for every table:

Seats = (diameter × 3.14) ÷ 24 inches For a 60-inch table: 60 × 3.14 = 188 inches of edge, divided by 24, is 7.85 — so eight chairs, and the arithmetic is telling you the eighth is already snug.

These match what rental suppliers publish, which is a good sign: the formula is not a theory, it is the same arithmetic the industry already runs.

What the tenth chair costs

Put ten people at a 60-inch table and each one gets 18.8 inches of edge instead of 23.6. That is the difference between a comfortable place setting and one where the water glasses touch and someone has to lean back so their neighbour can cut.

It is worth it when:

  • the room simply will not hold more tables
  • the meal is buffet or family-style, so there are fewer glasses and plates
  • the table has no centrepiece competing for the middle

It is not worth it when:

  • there is a plated dinner with more than two courses
  • the centrepiece is tall or wide
  • the guests at that table include anyone who will struggle to get in and out

Don't forget the floor

Fitting guests at tables is only half of it. Tables also need space between them: two chairs backing onto each other take about 18 inches each, and a person carrying two plates needs roughly two feet to pass between them. That comes to about five feet from table edge to table edge — and it is the number people forget when they work out how many tables a room holds.

A 60-inch table therefore occupies a circle roughly 10 feet across once you include its chairs and the walkway around it, not 5 feet.

Round or rectangular?

A 6-foot banquet table (30 × 72 inches) seats six to eight, and an 8-foot one seats eight to ten — similar numbers per table, but the two shapes behave differently. Round tables let everyone see everyone, which is why they dominate weddings. Long tables fit more people into a narrow room and look better in photographs, but a guest can only really talk to four people all evening.

Questions people ask

Can a 60-inch table seat 12?

Physically yes, at 15.7 inches each — narrower than a dining chair. Nobody will eat comfortably. Use a 72-inch table instead.

How many 60-inch tables do I need for 150 guests?

Nineteen at eight per table, or fifteen at ten. Add one for anyone you have forgotten, because you have forgotten someone.

Does the head table follow the same rule?

No. A head table seats on one side only, so it needs about 24 inches of length per person rather than per edge — a 12-foot head table takes six.

What about children?

Two children fit where one adult would at the squeeze, but only if they are at the same table and small. Planning them as adults and being pleasantly surprised beats the reverse.
